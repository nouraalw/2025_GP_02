1. Introduction
The transition from education to professional life is a crucial phase that defines an
individual’s career path. In Saudi Arabia, this challenge has become a key focus as the country
works towards Vision 2030, emphasizing the empowerment of youth by bridging the gap
between academic education and professional development. However, many students and
graduates face challenges during this transition due to the lack of personalized career guidance
and targeted skill-building opportunities.
This project addresses the issue of insufficient mentorship and career guidance available to
students and graduates. Without proper support, many struggle to enter the workforce with
confidence, leading to a skills gap that impacts their ability to effectively participate in the job
market.
To tackle this, we propose the development of a web-based platform called ASPIRA,
designed to provide personalized mentorship and career development support through
technology. By leveraging artificial intelligence, the platform aims to enhance accessibility and
offer tailored guidance through features such as AI-powered chatbot, recommendation system,
and a unified hub connecting mentees with professional mentors. This platform aims to equip
students and graduates with the skills, confidence, and direction they need to succeed in their
careers.
This document will provide a detailed description of the problem being addressed, outline
the proposed solution, and include details about the technologies used, methodologies adopted,
and expected outcomes of the project, aligning the initiative with the objectives of our Vision.

1.1.

The Problem

In Saudi Arabia, a significant real-world problem persists: many mentees, particularly
bachelor’s students and recent graduates, struggle to transition effectively from education to
the workforce. This challenge is particularly critical because it affects their ability to contribute
meaningfully to the country's growth and long-term objectives.
For example, consider a university graduate who, despite excelling academically, finds it
difficult to identify the right career path or acquire the skills needed for specific roles in the
competitive job market. The lack of accessible and personalized career guidance often leaves
individuals like this graduate unprepared to navigate the complexities of professional
development.
Our project focuses on addressing a critical part of this issue: the absence of tailored
mentorship and skill-building opportunities. Many mentees lack regular access to professional
mentors who can provide career advice tailored to their goals and interests. This gap hinders
their ability to plan effectively for their careers, build confidence, and acquire relevant skills.
By bridging this gap, we aim to empower youth to achieve their professional potential and
align with national aspirations for a skilled and competitive workforce.

1.2.

The Solution

This project is designed to revolutionize career mentorship by creating a smart, AI-powered
platform that bridges the gap between mentees and professional mentors. The goal is to provide
personalized guidance, structured mentorship, and tailored learning resources, helping mentees
navigate their career paths with confidence. Unlike traditional systems where finding the right
mentor can be difficult and guidance is often generalized, this platform ensures that mentees
are matched with mentors who align with their interests, career aspirations, and expertise needs.
The platform's Mentor Management system, handled by an admin, ensures that only
qualified and verified mentors are approved, maintaining a high standard of mentorship. To
make mentorship more accessible and effective, mentees can search for and filter mentors
based on criteria such as industry and years of experience, ensuring a personalized experience.
Once connected, mentees can book private one-on-one mentorship sessions or participate in
interactive group sessions through the Zegocloud API [1], enabling seamless video
communication for career discussions, skill-building, and guidance. Mentees can reschedule or
cancel their bookings, while mentors have the ability to adjust or remove sessions as needed.
To ensure clear communication, mentees will receive a notification whenever a mentor
reschedules or cancels a booked private session. This keeps them informed about any changes
and allows them to manage their schedules effectively.To enhance the learning experience,
each session is documented, with mentors responsible for writing summaries so mentees can
review key takeaways at any time.
Beyond mentorship sessions, the platform actively supports mentees’ career growth
through an AI-powered recommendation system that suggests relevant courses based on user
interests, ensuring they develop skills aligned with their professional goals. Additionally, an
AI-powered chatbot, integrated using OpenAI’s [2] technology, provides instant assistance
with platform navigation, session management, and career-related inquiries, ensuring mentees
always have access to the information they need.
Mentees and mentors have full control over their profiles, allowing them to update their
information, as their careers progress. To further support mentees in their professional growth,
the platform includes a CV Builder, which helps mentees create a professional CV by entering

generate CV for job applications and networking opportunities.
By combining AI-driven learning, structured mentorship, real-time interactions, and a
strong community-driven approach, this platform provides an essential resource for career
development. It directly supports Vision 2030 by equipping Saudi youth with the skills,
mentorship, and guidance they need to thrive in a competitive job market. By fostering a culture

of professionals, contributing to national growth, economic innovation, and workforce

mentors who align with their career goals, interests, and fields of study. It provides a structured

platform, not an educational course provider. While it offers intelligent course
recommendations, it does not handle course registration, enrollment, or payment processing.
Its primary mission is to foster meaningful mentor-mentee connections, support career growth,
and provide structured, AI-enhanced guidance.

2.1 Career Advising
Career advice plays a vital role in both personal and professional development, providing
mentees with the support and guidance they need to navigate their education and career choices.
It’s a collaborative process where mentors help mentees discover their strengths, set realistic
goals, and make informed decisions about their future. Through structured guidance, career
advising enables mentees to explore different career options, assess their abilities, and develop
the skills necessary to excel in their chosen fields. Career mentors play a crucial role in this
journey, helping mentees identify their skills and interests to ensure they choose career paths
that align with their capabilities and aspirations. [7]
This background discussion will explore the core aspects of career advice, how it helps
mentees unlock their potential, and its impact on making well-informed career decisions
throughout their professional journeys.

2.1.1 Career Advising Definition and Significance
Career advice serves an essential role in helping mentees navigate their professional
journeys. At its core, career advice is a process of guidance and mentorship aimed at
empowering mentees to make informed decisions about their future. It encompasses a range of
activities, from identifying strengths and setting achievable goals to exploring opportunities
and overcoming challenges.
The significance of career advice lies in its ability to provide clarity and direction during
important stages of a person's life. Whether someone is choosing a field of study, entering the
job market, or considering a career change, sound advice can make all the difference. It helps
mentees understand their unique skills, interests, and potential while aligning these with market
demands and opportunities.[8]
Career advice is a flexible and tailored approach focused on building a strong relationship
between mentors and mentees.This approach creates a supportive environment where mentees
can reflect on their aspirations, evaluate their options, and develop strategies for success. By
offering insights into career trends and industry expectations, mentors help mentees navigate
the complexities of the job market and align their decisions with long-term goals. [ 9]
24

In addition to providing guidance, career advice equips mentees with practical tools such as
crafting resumes, preparing for interviews, exploring career transitions, and understanding
industry-specific trends. It also delivers actionable strategies to address career-related
challenges, from managing competition to building professional networks. By combining
personalized mentorship with practical support, career advice empowers mentees to
confidently pursue their aspirations in their professional journeys.
Also career advice fosters personal growth. It helps mentees build confidence, adaptability,
and problem-solving skills, which are essential in a constantly evolving job market. By offering
guidance on time management, work-life balance, and professional development, career advice
ensures that mentees are prepared to face challenges and achieve long-term success.[10]

2.1.2 Types of Career Advising and Challenges in Career Advising

Career advising includes various approaches designed to support mentees at different
stages of their professional journey. The most common types include directive advising,
assessment-based advising, and specialized advising, each offering unique methods to guide
career decision-making with the help of mentors. However, despite the availability of these
approaches, mentees often encounter significant challenges in navigating their career paths.
Understanding both the types of career advising and the obstacles mentees face is crucial in
providing effective guidance and support.
● Directive Advising:
Directive advising is a hands-on approach where mentors provide clear, specific guidance
to help mentees make confident career decisions. Rather than exploring multiple options,
mentors offer direct solutions and practical strategies for tasks such as choosing between job
offers, improving resumes, or preparing for interviews. This approach is particularly beneficial
for mentees who need quick answers or prefer straightforward advice from a mentor. [11]
However, many mentees face challenges that hinder their career progress, such as uncertainty
about their career direction and a lack of personalized guidance. Where many struggle to
identify which career path suits them best, what opportunities align with their skills, or how to
25

take the next step, leaving them feeling lost. Additionally, generic career advice often fails to
consider a mentee’s unique strengths, interests, and aspirations, making it difficult to apply
broad recommendations to their specific situations.[12]

●

Assessment-Based Advising:
relies on personality tests, aptitude assessments, and skills evaluations to help mentees

understand their strengths and align them with suitable career paths. This data-driven approach
provides valuable insights into professions that match their abilities and interests, guiding them
toward informed career decisions.[11] However, mentees often face challenges that complicate
career planning, such as skills gap issues and the rapidly changing job market. Many job
seekers, especially graduates, struggle with misalignment between their skills and employer
expectations, making it difficult to secure job opportunities or advance professionally.
Additionally, as industries evolve with new technologies and shifting demands, mentees must
continuously assess their competencies and determine whether further training or education is
needed to remain competitive.[12]
● Specialized Career Advising:
Specialized career advising provides a personalized approach for mentees facing unique
career challenges. Specialized mentors support students exploring career paths, young
professionals seeking growth, career changers navigating transitions, and individuals
overcoming obstacles such as workplace discrimination or skill gaps. By understanding each
mentee’s specific situation, these mentors offer tailored guidance to help them progress in their
careers. [11]However, many mentees struggle with self-doubt and fear of failure, especially

2.2 Chatbots: An overview
Chatbots have become an integral part of our everyday interactions with technology,
fundamentally changing how we communicate with systems and access information. These
28

virtual assistants are designed to simulate human-like conversations, making communication
feel intuitive and natural. At the core of chatbots lies Natural Language Processing (NLP), a
branch of artificial intelligence that enables them to understand, process, and respond to human
language in meaningful ways. Over the years, chatbots have evolved significantly, transitioning
from basic systems with limited capabilities to advanced tools that can engage in complex,
context-aware interactions.
There are three primary types of chatbots, each suited to different needs. First, Rule-Based
Chatbots operate based on predefined rules or scripts. These are the simplest form of chatbots,
following decision trees to guide users through fixed workflows. They work well for
straightforward tasks like answering frequently asked questions or providing basic information
but struggle when users deviate from their programmed paths. Second, AI-Powered Chatbots
take things to the next level. These bots use machine learning and natural language
understanding to interpret user input, learn from interactions, and adapt to context. They are
capable of dynamic conversations, making them ideal for handling more complex queries,
providing personalized recommendations, or even assisting with problem-solving. Finally,
Hybrid Chatbots combine the best of both worlds, using rule-based logic for simple tasks while
leveraging AI for more intricate or unpredictable scenarios. This balance allows them to handle
a broader range of interactions while maintaining efficiency.
Despite their growing capabilities, chatbots come with both strengths and challenges.
Among their most significant advantages is their ability to provide 24/7 support, ensuring users
receive help at any time without delays. They also excel at scalability, managing multiple
conversations simultaneously, which is invaluable for businesses looking to handle high
volumes of queries. Moreover, their ability to personalize interactions based on user data
creates a more engaging and relevant experience for individuals. This is particularly useful in
industries like e-commerce, customer service, and healthcare, where personalization can
significantly enhance satisfaction and loyalty. Additionally, chatbots can help businesses save
costs by automating repetitive tasks and reducing the need for human intervention in routine
inquiries.
However, like any technology, chatbots are not without their challenges. Rule-based
chatbots, while efficient for simple tasks, often fail to handle more nuanced or open-ended

29

queries, leading to user frustration. For more advanced, AI-powered chatbots, the development
process can be highly complex, requiring expertise in artificial intelligence, machine learning,
and natural language processing. Another significant concern is privacy and data security,
particularly when chatbots handle sensitive information, such as in healthcare or finance.
Striking the right balance between personalization and protecting user data is an ongoing
challenge for developers and businesses alike.

2.2.1 OpenAI Chatbots
Natural Language Processing (NLP) has transitioned from relying on task-specific

question answering, and textual entailment. Although the foundational architecture and initial
representations are now task-agnostic, a final task-specific adaptation step remains necessary.
This involves fine-tuning the pre-trained model on a large dataset of examples, ensuring it is
optimized for a specific task. [15]

2.3 Recommendation systems
In today’s world, where we are bombarded with endless choices—be it movies, courses, job
listings, or products—recommendation systems have become essential. These smart systems
help users find what they need by analyzing their preferences, behaviors, and past interactions.
Whether it’s Netflix suggesting your next binge-worthy show, an online learning platform
recommending courses that match your career goals, or an e-commerce site curating a shopping
list just for you, recommendation systems are shaping the way we interact with digital content.

32

2.3.1 How Do Recommendation Systems Work
At their core, recommendation systems use machine learning and statistical analysis to
predict what a user might like based on various factors. They act like digital assistants, learning
from your interactions to make increasingly accurate suggestions over time. The goal is to cut
through the noise and present options that are truly relevant to you. [17]

2.3.2 Types of Recommendation Systems
Recommendation systems can be categorized into different types based on their underlying
methodologies. Content-based filtering systems analyze item attributes and user preferences to
suggest similar content, making them suitable for applications like movie recommendations.
Collaborative filtering, on the other hand, relies on user behavior patterns, identifying groups
with similar interests to generate suggestions. Hybrid systems integrate multiple approaches to
enhance recommendation accuracy, often combining content-based and collaborative
techniques. Finally, knowledge-based systems use predefined rules and expert knowledge to
recommend items that match specific user needs, making them particularly effective in
domains like job matching and personalized services. [18]

2.3.3 Applications of Recommendation Systems
Without recommendation systems, navigating the overwhelming number of choices
available online would be exhausting. These systems make life easier by providing
personalized experiences across different platforms:
● Streaming Services (like YouTube): They analyze your watching or listening habits to
suggest content you’ll likely enjoy.
● E-Commerce (Amazon, eBay): They track your browsing and purchasing history to
recommend products that match your interests.
● Online Learning (Coursera, Udacity, edX): They suggest courses based on your skills,
career goals, and past learning history.
● Job Portals (like LinkedIn): They match job seekers with relevant job listings based
on their resumes, searches, and application history.

33

2.3.4 The Tech Behind Recommendation Systems
Recommendation systems use a combination of machine learning techniques to provide
accurate suggestions tailored to users' preferences and needs. One key method is Natural
Language Processing (NLP), which enables systems to analyze and interpret text-based data,
such as course descriptions, job postings, and movie summaries, to make meaningful
recommendations. Another important technique is TF-IDF (Term Frequency-Inverse
Document Frequency), a mathematical approach that assesses the significance of words within
a document. For example, in job recommendation systems, TF-IDF helps match keywords
from a user's resume with those found in job descriptions, improving relevance. Additionally,
Cosine Similarity plays a crucial role by measuring the similarity between two documents or
sets of information. This technique is often used to compare a user's past interactions with new
content, ensuring that recommendations align with their preferences. When combined, these
techniques enhance the precision of recommendation systems by analyzing patterns and
relationships within data.
With the rapid growth of digital information, users often face information overload, making
it challenging to filter relevant content, particularly in news portals where articles are published
massively. Readers need time to search for and read news, but the time relevance of articles
diminishes quickly. To address this, recommendation systems play a crucial role in delivering
personalized content efficiently. A notable study implemented a news recommendation system
using the TF-IDF method, which assigns weights to each word in the news title and then applies
cosine similarity to find similarities between stories. To evaluate the system’s effectiveness,
the researchers compared the recommended articles with readers' actual news history on the
Microsoft News portal using a hit-rate metric, achieving an accuracy of 80.77%, indicating the
strong relevance of the system's recommendations.
This demonstrates how TF-IDF and Cosine Similarity are effective in various domains,
from job matching to personalized news recommendations, enhancing user experience by
delivering highly relevant content.[19]
Recommendation systems offer significant business benefits by leveraging data filtering
tools to drive revenue growth, enabling cross-selling and upselling through personalized
recommendations. By presenting relevant suggestions, customers are more likely to discover
34

and purchase additional products, leading to increased sales. Additionally, these systems
enhance customer satisfaction by tailoring recommendations to user preferences, making the
platform feel more responsive and engaging, which fosters retention and a positive user
experience. With personalization at scale, recommendation engines analyze customer data to
create individualized profiles, increasing conversion rates by curating products specifically
suited to each user. Furthermore, these systems generate informative reports that provide
insights into customer behavior, preferences, and trends, allowing businesses to make datadriven decisions for continuous growth. They also reduce workload by automating
recommendations, freeing employees from manually curating suggestions and enabling them
to focus on other strategic tasks. Lastly, controlled retailing helps businesses showcase new or
underexposed products, manage inventory effectively, and strategically influence purchasing
behavior, ultimately driving targeted sales and optimizing retail performance.[20]

recommendation systems face several key challenges. One major challenge is fostering
communities of interest while ensuring user privacy is respected. Balancing personalization
with data protection remains a crucial concern. Another significant challenge is developing
recommendation algorithms capable of integrating multiple data sources collected at different
times. For newly registered users, personal information can be gathered during registration,
while contextual data such as location and time can be inferred from their IP address.
Additionally, recommendations are often influenced by items that have been frequently
viewed, downloaded, or purchased by others, further shaping the system’s ability to deliver
relevant suggestions.[21]
While recommendation systems have significantly enhanced content discovery, they still
face notable challenges. One major issue is the cold start problem, where new users or items
lack sufficient interaction history, making accurate recommendations difficult. Additionally,
data bias can arise if the training data is skewed, leading to recommendations that
disproportionately favor certain types of content. Another challenge is over-personalization,
where users are repeatedly shown content similar to their past interactions, limiting exposure
to diverse recommendations and potentially reinforcing filter bubbles.

5.1 Architecture
The Client-Server architecture has been selected as the most suitable design for the
ASPIRA platform, as it provides centralized data management and ensures effective
communication between users (mentees, mentors, and admin) and the server. The server is
responsible for processing all client requests, including searching for mentors by name,
filtering by field, scheduling sessions, recommending relevant courses, and reviewing mentor
registration requests submitted by mentors for approval or rejection by the admin. It also
connects to the database to store and retrieve information before sending the results back to the
clients using the appropriate protocol.
This architecture significantly simplifies data management through centralized
administration, allowing the storage of all critical information such as mentor registration
statuses in a single database, making it easier for the admin to review and manage the system
efficiently. Additionally, all records remain easily accessible since they are maintained on the
central server, allowing for immediate data retrieval when needed.
This architecture also ensures fast response times when searching for mentors or filtering
results, as the server quickly processes and returns data without requiring local storage on user
devices, improving platform performance. Moreover, it provides strong security and data
protection, ensuring that sensitive information such as registration requests, CVs, and session
details is stored in a secure location rather than being distributed across multiple devices,
reducing the risk of data loss or unauthorized access.
Furthermore, the Client-Server architecture offers scalability, allowing the server capacity
to be expanded easily to handle increased requests as the number of users grows, ensuring
system stability and reliability even with higher traffic.
Based on these factors, the Client-Server architecture is the ideal choice for ASPIRA in
terms of performance, reliability, and ease of use, enabling users (mentees, mentors, and
admin) to interact with the platform efficiently while ensuring a smooth and dependable
experience for all.

6.2.3 ZEGOCLOUD API

ZEGOCLOUD is a real-time audio and video communication service that provides
developers with powerful SDKs and APIs to build seamless video call, voice chat, and live
streaming features into their applications. It offers low latency, high-quality media
transmission, and a wide range of customizable UI components to suit different use cases from
online meetings and virtual classrooms to one-on-one consultations. In this project,
ZEGOCLOUD is used to power private video sessions between mentors and mentees, allowing
for secure, interactive communication without the need to build a video infrastructure from
scratch. By integrating ZEGOCLOUD, the platform ensures a smooth, reliable, and scalable
video calling experience.
Figure 30 illustrates the process of initializing the video call session. The system first
retrieves the roomID from the URL using the $_GET superglobal. This roomID uniquely
identifies the virtual meeting room associated with a scheduled session. Using this identifier,
the backend executes a prepared SQL statement to fetch the scheduled time and date of the
session from the sessions table.

Figure 30 retrieve Room ID

session's end time is computed to determine how many seconds remain. This remaining time
is stored in the $time_remaining variable and later used in the JavaScript to automatically end
the session when its duration expires. This mechanism ensures that sessions follow strict timing
rules and helps maintain order and reliability across all scheduled video meetings.

One of the primary uses of PHPMailer is to send OTP (One-Time Password) verification
codes during the registration process for both mentors and mentees. After entering their email
address, the user receives a code via email which they must enter to complete their registration.
This ensures that the email address is valid and helps maintain platform security.
PHPMailer is also used in the password reset process. When a user forgets their password,
they can request a reset link, which is sent to their email. This link contains a secure token and
redirects the user to the reset password page, where they can safely enter and set a new
password.
In addition, PHPMailer is used to notify mentors about the status of their application. If a
mentor is approved, they receive a confirmation email welcoming them to the platform. If the
application is rejected, a polite rejection message is sent explaining that their request was not
accepted at this time.

111

Through these features, PHPMailer plays a key role in improving user experience,
strengthening platform security, and maintaining effective communication between the system
and its users.

6.3 Implementation Challenges
Throughout the implementation process, we encountered several challenges and worked to
overcome them, including:

● User Authentication and Email Verification:
One of the main challenges was building a secure sign-up system for both mentors and
mentees, along with implementing OTP email verification for mentor and mentee
registration. We used PHPMailer to send verification emails, and we faced some difficulties
with its configuration such as email delays and deliverability issues. Ensuring a smooth
the verification process without confusing the user required multiple tests and adjustments.

● User Interface Design and Usability:
Designing an interface that is both intuitive and functional for two different user types
(mentors and mentees) posed several challenges. Balancing simplicity with features like
session booking, ratings, and filters wasn’t easy. We had to make careful design choices to
keep things clear and easy to use without losing any important features.

112

● Rating System and Comment Handling:
Creating a flexible and dynamic rating system with comment support involved
designing a UI that locks after submission, prevents duplicate ratings, and updates the
mentor’s average score in real time. Ensuring that this logic worked consistently
across multiple pages required extra care in both frontend and backend development.

● Database Design and Interactions:
Designing the database to handle the relationships between users, sessions, and ratings was
a challenge. We had to make sure the data stayed accurate and that everything worked
smoothly when updating the interface in real time.

● Filtering and Search Functionality:
Implementing filtering and search features in the Mentor Center was more complicated
than expected. We needed to allow mentees to easily find mentors based on specific
fields and years of experience, without slowing down the platform. Making sure the
filters worked accurately and updated results quickly required extra work on both the
frontend and backend.

8.

Conclusions and Future Work
The integration of artificial intelligence within mentorship and career development reaches
its greatest milestone through ASPIRA. ASPIRA was established with Saudi Arabia's youth
support in mind to provide an organized scalable platform that links mentees with experienced
mentors while supplying necessary tools for professional growth.
We built the system to respond to genuine academic-to-workplace transition problems faced
by students and recent graduates. Career-based problems that youth face are their restricted
access to mentorship services combined with uncertain career paths and weak skills training
materials. The platform of ASPIRA includes multiple features which combine one-on-one
mentorship sessions with group mentorship sessions while delivering AI-based educational
recommendations to users in addition to providing an automatic CV builder and an intelligent
chatbot for instant help.
The development work combined different fields of expertise which included user research
together with requirement engineering along with literature reviews and architectural design
and advanced software development methods. Each aspect of the platform development
depended on user feedback to create a practical solution which continues providing relevant
and usable features. Multiple rounds of development along with strategic plans and joint work
efforts transformed ASPIRA into its current status as a modern digital mentorship platform
meeting today's educational and professional criteria.

8.1 Global and local impact
The local impact of ASPIRA in Saudi Arabia is profound. The platform directly aligns with
Vision 2030 by enabling Saudi youth to become job-market ready, develop soft and hard skills,
and access meaningful mentorship without geographical barriers. ASPIRA fosters a supportive
ecosystem where mentees gain confidence, explore new career paths, and build personal
development strategies with the help of mentors who bring industry expertise.
Through the AI-powered features, ASPIRA not only increases access to mentorship but also
personalizes the experience, ensuring each mentee is matched with the most suitable mentor
127

based on their interests and goals. This leads to more effective outcomes, higher satisfaction,
and better preparedness for professional life.
On a global scale, ASPIRA has the potential to serve as a model for other countries facing
similar gaps in career support systems. By combining mentorship with AI technologies like
recommendation systems and chatbots, ASPIRA introduces a scalable framework that can be
localized for different regions. It serves as an example of how technology can democratize
access to professional guidance, enabling students and early-career professionals to succeed,
regardless of background or resources.
Furthermore, ASPIRA contributes to the global conversation on digital education and
workforce readiness, offering insights into how technology can support lifelong learning,
personalized development, and skill alignment with evolving job markets.

8.2 Problems and challenges encountered during the software
development
Throughout the software development life cycle, the ASPIRA team encountered various
technical and operational challenges, which required agile problem-solving and continuous
learning. These challenges included:
1. API Integration and Time Constraints
During development, a key external API unexpectedly became unavailable, disrupting
platform progress. The team had to invest time in finding a reliable alternative and updating
the integration logic, which introduced delays and added technical complexity.
2. Mentor and Mentee Role Separation
A critical bug was identified where booked sessions incorrectly recorded the mentee ID
as the mentor ID. Fixing this required restructuring backend logic and updating database
queries to ensure role integrity.

128

3. Rating System Integration
Implementing a one-time, non-editable 5-star rating system with comment support
demanded careful design. Preventing duplicate submissions and dynamically updating
mentor averages required advanced state handling.

4. User Experience Consistency
Creating a consistent, intuitive interface for mentors, mentees, and admins
required multiple iterations. Balancing feature complexity with ease of use was a key
design challenge.

5. Database Optimization
As features grew, so did the complexity of database interactions. The team worked on
optimizing SQL queries, preventing redundancy, and ensuring referential integrity across
tables such as users, sessions, ratings.

8.3 Limitations of the system

As with any project, ASPIRA has a number of limitations, many of which have been
identified for future enhancement:
● Language Support
Currently, ASPIRA supports English only, which may exclude Arabic-speaking users or
non-English speakers from fully utilizing the platform.

129

● API Call Limitations
ASPIRA’s video session feature relies on a third-party API that provides a limited
number of free calling minutes per month. This restriction could impact session
availability or quality if usage exceeds the quota, especially during periods of high
activity.

● Mobile Responsiveness
While the platform is accessible via browser, full optimization for mobile devices is still
under development, potentially affecting the experience on small screens.

● Lack of In-App Messaging
Communication between mentors and mentees is limited to session time. There is no realtime chat feature to enable discussions outside scheduled meetings.

8.4 The main contribution of the project
ASPIRA offers several distinct contributions to the field of educational technology and
career development:
1. End-to-End Mentorship Platform
○ ASPIRA offers a full ecosystem for career guidance, from mentor discovery
and booking to session summaries, feedback, and real-time communication.

130

2. AI-Powered Chatbot
○ The integration of a GPT-4-based chatbot offers mentees real-time, contextaware responses to common career and platform-related questions, increasing
accessibility and user engagement.
3. Course Recommendation Engine
○ Using a content-based filtering approach, the system suggests tailored learning
materials that support mentees' career paths, ensuring continuous growth
outside of mentorship sessions.
4. CV Builder
o A guided CV builder to help mentees create professional resumes with ease,
supporting their job applications and career development.

8.5 Future work
To enhance ASPIRA and expand its impact, the team has identified the following areas
for future development:
1. Arabic Language Support
○ Translate and localize the entire platform interface and chatbot to support
bilingual users in Saudi Arabia and other Arabic-speaking countries.

131

2. Mobile Application Development
○ Launch dedicated iOS and Android apps with optimized interfaces for mobile
use, ensuring greater accessibility and smoother user experience.
3. Real-Time Messaging System
○ Add secure, instant chat functionality between mentors and mentees to allow
ongoing communication, file sharing, and scheduling support.

4. Calendar Integration and Session Reminders
○ Sync booked sessions with Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, or Outlook,
with automatic reminders via email or push notification.
5. Speech-Based Chatbot Support
○ Enhance the chatbot to include voice interaction features, especially useful for
users with disabilities or those preferring hands-free usage.
6. Automated Mentor Onboarding
○ Implement AI-powered CV parsing and scoring to assist admins in evaluating
mentor applications and reduce manual workload.
